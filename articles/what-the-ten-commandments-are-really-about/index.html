What the Ten Commandments Are Really About

7 min read

Say "the Ten Commandments" and most people picture a list of prohibitions carved in stone — a set of rules to keep, an ancient code of do's and don'ts. It's a fair first impression. It's also incomplete.

In their original setting, the ten words God speaks at Mount Sinai aren't primarily a legal code. They're the opening terms of a relationship — a portrait of what life looks like for a people who have just been rescued.

Rescue comes first

Notice how the commandments begin. Before a single rule is given, God says: "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of Egypt, out of the land of slavery." The commandments don't earn that rescue. They follow it.

That order changes everything. This isn't a list you keep to win God's favor. It's a description of how free people live — people who no longer belong to Pharaoh.

The commandments aren't the price of the relationship. They're the shape of it.

A portrait of the good life

Read together, the ten words sketch a whole way of being. The first few reorient our loves — no rival gods, no idols, honoring God's name and rhythm of rest. The rest protect what makes human community flourish — honoring family, guarding life, faithfulness, honesty, contentment.

It's less a fence and more a trellis: a structure that lets a good life grow.

Where they point

The deeper you go, the more the commandments reveal how far our hearts drift from them — and how much we need more than a list. That tension is intentional. The Law was always pointing beyond itself, toward the one who would fulfill it and write it on human hearts.

That's the story the whole series traces, one commandment at a time.
